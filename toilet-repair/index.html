Toilet repair in Fernley covers clogs, running tanks, weak flushes, loose bowls and leaks at the base or the supply line. Many repairs are simple parts swaps, and when a toilet is cracked or very old, we explain when replacement is the better choice.

Urgent situations

If the toilet is overflowing right now

Overflows can be stopped in a minute. These steps help.

Stop the water

Reach behind the toilet and turn the shutoff valve clockwise. If it is stuck, lift the float in the tank to stop the fill.

Do not flush again

A second flush adds water to a full bowl. Wait for a plumber or try a plunger once the level drops.

Contain the spill

Put towels around the base and keep children and pets away. Wastewater is unsanitary.

Check other drains

If the tub or sink also drains slowly, the clog may be in the main line. Stop using water and say so when you call.

Diagnosis

Common toilet problems and fixes

Most toilet problems have a short list of causes.

If the same toilet clogs again soon after clearing, the problem may be in the line behind it.

Running toilets

A running toilet wastes more than you think

A toilet that refills every few minutes can waste a lot of water over a month, and you may not notice until the bill arrives. According to the EPA WaterSense program, a food coloring test is the simple way to find a silent leak. Add a few drops of food coloring to the tank and wait ten minutes without flushing. If color shows in the bowl, the flapper is leaking.

This is a quick fix in most cases. If the bill is high and the toilets are fine, see leak detection to look for a hidden line leak.

Clogs

Clogs: when a plunger is enough and when it is not

Not every clog is the toilet. Where else water backs up tells you.

One toilet only

A plunger or toilet auger usually clears it. Use a flange plunger, which seals better than a flat cup.

Toilet and tub both slow

The clog is likely in the branch line. See drain cleaning.

A toy or hard object

If something solid was flushed, the toilet may need to be pulled to get it out.

Repeated clogs

Low-flow toilets of any age can clog if the trap or line is partly blocked. A camera look can show why.

Repair or replace a toilet

A toilet with a cracked bowl or a leak from the porcelain should be replaced. A toilet that is old and needs a new flange, wax ring and fill valve may also be cheaper to replace than to keep repairing.

If you are replacing it, we confirm the rough-in size, check the floor flange, set the new bowl on a fresh wax ring and test for leaks. For other fixture work, see faucet repair.

Business sites

Toilets in commercial and shared buildings

A failing toilet in a shop, office or warehouse bathroom may affect staff and customers. After-hours calls can be handled by the 24/7 line, and for larger bathroom groups see commercial plumbing.

Shops along Main Street and warehouses in the Victory Logistics District share one common cause of restroom clogs, which is paper towels and wipes flushed down the bowl. A sign in the restroom and a trash receptacle help.

Cost factors

What affects the price of toilet repair

The price depends on the part, whether the toilet has to be pulled and reset, and the time of the call. A flapper swap is a smaller job than a flange repair.

You get a clear price before work starts, and we say if replacement makes more sense than repeated repairs.
