Leak detection in Fernley uses listening equipment, thermal imaging and pressure tests to pinpoint where water is escaping. Finding the leak first means we open the smallest possible area, which keeps repair cost and repair mess down.

Urgent situations

What to do while a leak is active

Time matters with a leak. These steps limit damage until the plumber arrives.

Shut off the main valve

Know where the main shutoff is. Turn it fully closed to stop water flow while you wait.

Check the meter

With every fixture off, a moving meter dial or low-flow indicator means water is going somewhere. Take a photo of the reading.

Protect belongings

Move furniture, lift rugs and place towels. Water that spreads under flooring is hard to dry out.

Stay clear of wet electrical

If water reaches outlets or the panel, do not touch it. Turn off power at the breaker if you can do so safely, and tell the dispatcher.

Methods

How hidden leaks are found

We use more than one method because each has limits. A cross-check keeps us from opening the wrong wall.

Acoustic listening

Sensitive microphones pick up the sound of water escaping from a pressurized pipe. Moving the sensor along a wall or floor narrows the spot to a few inches.

Thermal imaging

Hot water leaks leave warm patches and wet material cools differently. A thermal camera shows both, and it works without touching the surface.

Pressure testing

Isolating a section of line and watching the gauge tells us whether that section holds. It sorts supply leaks from drain issues and narrows where to look.

Service lines

Water service lines and meter leaks

Some leaks are outside the house. Knowing which side of the meter you are on affects who pays.

The line from the meter to the house

This underground pipe is usually the homeowner's responsibility. A leak here can run for months with no surface sign.

Reading the meter

Shut every fixture off and watch the meter. A moving dial confirms a leak and a still dial suggests the problem is a fixture or the bill itself.

City or private side

The City of Fernley Utility Billing office manages accounts, and its number is 775-784-9930. Ask where the city's responsibility ends before you plan a repair.

Why meters matter for high bills

A leak that runs all day raises the bill even when the house looks dry. If the bill jumps, a meter check is the cheapest first step.

Local conditions

Leaks in a cold desert climate

Fernley sits in a cold desert, with winter lows around the low 20s in January and a dry summer. Pipes in attics, crawlspaces and exterior walls can freeze, expand and split. A split pipe may only drip until the next thaw, then leak harder.

If you find damage after a freeze, shut off the main and call. A plumber can check for more than one split and test the line once repairs are made. Burst pipes are handled on our burst pipe repair page.

Cost factors

What affects the price of leak detection

The price depends on how many methods are needed, how large the property is and whether the leak is above ground, in a wall or under a slab. Access to the plumbing and the time of day also matter.

You get a clear price before work starts. If detection finds a leak, we explain the repair options and their cost separately so you can decide.
