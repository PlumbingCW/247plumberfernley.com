Garbage Disposal Air Switch Repair for Fernley, NV Homes

The small button on your countertop is an air switch. When it stops working, the disposal may be fine, but the switch or its hose needs attention.

By Fernley 24/7 Plumbing · Published November 16, 2026 · Updated November 16, 2026

A garbage disposal air switch is a button mounted on the sink or countertop. When you press it, a puff of air travels through a thin hose to a small module under the sink, which switches the disposal on. There is no electrical wiring at the button, so it is safe near water.

When the switch fails, the disposal does not turn on. The usual causes are a loose or cracked hose, a worn air module or a loose connection. Many repairs take less than an hour, and often only the switch needs replacing.

Check these before calling

A few quick checks can save a service call.

  • Press the button and listen. A click from under the sink means the module works and the disposal may be jammed or tripped.
  • Press the reset button on the bottom of the disposal. If it clicks, try the air switch again.
  • Look under the sink. The thin hose should run from the button to the module without kinks or cracks.
  • Check that the module is plugged in and that the outlet has power. A tripped GFCI or breaker can look like a switch failure.
  • Do not reach into the disposal. Cut power first, always.

Process

How the switch is replaced

Replacement starts by cutting power to the disposal. The old button is removed by unscrewing the nut under the sink. The hose is pulled from the module and the new button and hose are fed through the hole and connected.

The module plugs into the outlet and the disposal plugs into the module. We test several presses and confirm that the disposal starts and stops reliably. It is a quick job when the sink hole fits the new button.

When the switch is not the problem

If the module clicks but the disposal does not run, the issue is in the disposal or its wiring. A jammed grinder or a failed motor is a different repair than a switch. If the disposal is old and leaks at the housing, replacement is the answer.

See our garbage disposal repair page for jams and leaks, and drain cleaning if the sink drains slowly behind the unit.

Care

Tips that keep the switch and disposal working

Run cold water for several seconds before and after you run the disposal. Feed scraps in slowly. Keep grease, fibrous scraps and large bones out of it. A clean grinder jams less often and puts less strain on the motor.

If the countertop button is wet inside, dry it. A water-filled hose can make the switch sluggish. Replace a hose that has kinked or aged and become stiff.

Parts

Choosing a replacement switch kit

The part is cheap. Getting the right one saves a second trip.

Match the module

Most air switch buttons work with most modules, but hose size and the connector differ. Take a photo of the module label before you shop.

Check the hole

A countertop button needs a hole of a certain diameter. A sink hole that is drilled too large may need a trim cover.

Consider a wall switch

If the air switch keeps failing, a standard wall switch wired by a licensed electrician is an alternative. It is a different kind of install with its own safety rules.

Cost sense

Why a plumber may suggest replacing the disposal at the same time

If the unit is old, jams often or has rust at the housing seam, a new air switch will not change how it performs. Pairing a new switch with a failing disposal can mean paying for two visits.

We tell you what we see and let you decide. A disposal that is only a few years old and works well deserves a simple switch swap. For installs and replacements, see garbage disposal repair.
