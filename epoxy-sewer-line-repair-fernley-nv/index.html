Epoxy Sewer Line Repair: A Fernley, NV Homeowner Guide

Epoxy lining makes a new pipe inside the old one. It can avoid digging, but only when the old pipe is still in shape.

By Fernley 24/7 Plumbing · Published November 16, 2026 · Updated November 16, 2026

Plumbing questions?.

Call Now

Epoxy sewer line repair, also called pipe lining or cured-in-place pipe, adds a resin-soaked liner inside an existing sewer pipe. The liner is inflated, pressed against the old pipe and cured until it hardens into a smooth, jointless pipe inside the old one. It seals cracks and root entry points without a long trench.

It does not fit every pipe. A collapsed line, a severe belly or a badly offset joint needs another repair. A camera inspection decides, and a good plumber will tell you honestly if lining is not the right answer.

Process

How a lining job runs

Most of the work happens at the cleanout and a small access point.

Clean the pipe

The line is cleaned by cable or jet so the liner can bond to the pipe wall.

Camera inspect

A camera confirms the pipe is round and in line. Breaks and sags are noted.

Insert the liner

A resin-soaked liner is pulled or inverted into the pipe and inflated against the wall.

Cure and open

Heat or light cures the resin. Service lines are reopened and a final camera pass confirms the result.

Questions to ask before agreeing to lining

A clear answer to each of these is a good sign.

  • Can I see the camera video of the pipe before and after cleaning?
  • Is the pipe still round and in line, or is there a sag or a collapse?
  • What is the diameter and length of the lined section, and how are lateral connections reopened?
  • What does the liner warranty cover, and who honors it?
  • Does the work require a permit, and who applies?
  • What happens if the liner does not fit?

When lining is a bad idea

If a pipe has collapsed or has a belly that holds water, lining locks in the flaw. If there are many broken joints and offsets, a replacement may last longer. If the pipe is far larger or smaller than a liner can fit, another method is needed.

Timeline

What to expect before, during and after the job

Before the lining, expect a cleaning pass and a camera inspection. You will see the camera video, and the plumber will explain whether the pipe qualifies. The work area is small, usually around the cleanout.

During curing, water use may be limited for several hours, so plan showers and laundry around it. After the liner cures, a camera pass confirms the result, and side connections are cut open from inside the pipe. Keep the final video, since it helps with warranty claims and a future sale.

Limits

Signs that lining may not be enough

A good plumber tells you when lining is not the answer.

Repeated backups after lining

A bellied section may still hold water. A repair, not a liner, solves that.

A cleanout that is hard to find

Lining needs access at both ends of the run. A missing cleanout adds cost.

Multiple connected lines

Branches that enter the main line are cut open after curing, and those joints need care.

A low spot in the yard

A sunken area above the line may mean a void. The soil may need attention before lining.

Price

Cost drivers to ask about

Lining cost depends on the length of the lined section, the pipe diameter, the number of connections and how much cleaning is needed first. Access matters as well, since a missing cleanout means more digging to create one.

Ask whether the quote covers the pre-cleaning, the camera work, the reinstatement of side connections and the final video. Ask what is not included. A written scope lets you compare bids fairly, and it avoids surprise charges once the job is underway.

Who owns the line and what permits apply in Fernley

In Fernley, the City of Fernley Utility Billing office at 775-784-9930 manages sewer accounts and can tell you where the city line ends and yours begins. Repairs on a sewer connection can require a permit, and the Building Department and Code Enforcement at 775-784-9900 can explain.

Silver Springs properties on the Lyon County Utilities sewer have their own connection rules. The office is at 34 Lakes Blvd in Dayton, phone (775) 246-6220. Our Silver Springs plumber page explains how we work there.
