A sewer camera inspection answers a question no one can answer by looking at a drain: what condition is the pipe in. If sewage is backing up right now, call the 24/7 line first and a plumber will clear the line and run the camera as part of the plan.

Sewer camera inspection in Fernley is useful before buying a home, after repeat clogs, when roots are suspected or before a repair is priced. We show you the video as it is recorded, mark the location of problems on the surface and explain what they mean.

Findings

What the camera shows

A good inspection is more than a video. It is a short report on what you saw.

Pipe material and condition

Plastic, clay, cast iron and other pipes each look different. Scale, cracks and corrosion are visible.

Roots, offsets and breaks

Roots appear as fibers or a mat. An offset shows as a step. A break shows soil or standing water.

Low spots and belly sections

Standing water in the line marks a sag, which collects waste and clogs again.

Inspection and what comes next

If the camera shows a sound pipe, you know that cleaning or no action is enough. If it shows damage, you can choose among options in sewer line repair. If the pipe is dirty but sound, sewer line cleaning is next.

We give you our honest view of what the video shows, and we explain the options without pushing a repair the pipe does not need.

Home purchase

Buying a home in Fernley

A camera inspection before closing can tell you about roots, offsets and breaks in a sewer lateral. This is helpful for older houses, and for any property with large trees near the line.

Ask the seller about cleanout locations and about the history of repairs. Homes served by septic instead of city sewer need a septic inspection as well. We can explain the difference and how an inspection fits your purchase timeline.

Responsibility

Rules and responsibility

The City of Fernley Utility Billing office manages sewer accounts and can tell you where the city's line ends and yours begins. Ask them at 775-784-9930. Repairs on a sewer connection can require a permit from the Building Department at 775-784-9900.

We provide a written summary of what we saw, which can help when you negotiate a purchase or file an insurance claim.

Cost factors

What affects the price of a sewer camera inspection

The price depends on the line length, the access, whether cleaning is needed before the camera can pass and the time of the call.

You get a clear price before work starts. If the line must be cleaned first, we explain that cost separately.
