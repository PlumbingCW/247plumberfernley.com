Emergency basics

If water is running, do these three things first

The first minutes decide how much damage follows. These steps are safe for anyone.

Shut off the main valve

Turn it clockwise until it stops. Find it before you need it, because many are near the water heater, the meter or the front of the house.

Turn off power and gas near water

Do not touch wet outlets or the panel. If you smell gas, leave the house and call the gas utility from outside.

Call and describe what you see

Tell us which fixtures are affected and whether water or sewage is involved. Photos help you remember details for the insurer.

Local conditions

What Fernley conditions mean for your plumbing

Local conditions shape which plumbing problems we see most.

A cold desert with hard freezes

Fernley sits in a cold desert, with January lows that average about 23 degrees and highs near 94 in July. Exposed pipes in garages, attics and outside walls are at risk each winter, and insulation is cheap compared to a flood.

The Truckee Canal and a wet spring

The Truckee Canal and the Newlands Project irrigate the area, and the city relies in part on canal seepage to recharge groundwater. A January 2008 canal levee breach flooded roughly 580 homes on the east side, so ground water is a real factor here. If your yard or crawlspace stays damp, ask for a leak check before assuming it is irrigation.

Highway towns and long runs

Fernley is a junction on I-80, US 50 Alternate and US 95A, and drive times to outlying properties vary. Call early and give a clear address so the plumber arrives with the right parts.

Licensing, permits and who to ask

In Nevada, plumbing and heating work falls under the C-1 classification overseen by the Nevada State Contractors Board. The board states that new and replacement water heaters require permits and inspections.

For permits inside Fernley, the City of Fernley Building Department and Code Enforcement answers at 775-784-9900 on weekdays. For water and sewer accounts, Utility Billing is at 775-784-9930. We explain what applies to your job before work starts.
