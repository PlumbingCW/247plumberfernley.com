13 kW Tankless Water Heater in Fernley, NV: What It Needs

A 13 kW unit sounds modest on paper. It draws a lot of power and heats only so much water at once.

By Fernley 24/7 Plumbing · Published November 16, 2026 · Updated November 16, 2026

A 13 kW electric tankless water heater uses 13,000 watts when it runs at full power. At 240 volts that is roughly 54 amps, so it needs a dedicated circuit and a breaker sized by the manufacturer and local code. It can serve one shower or a couple of low-flow fixtures in a mild climate, and less when incoming water is very cold.

Before you buy, check three things: the panel capacity, the flow you need and the temperature of your incoming water. A unit that is too small gives lukewarm water, and a panel that is too small makes the project far more expensive.

Flow

What 13 kW can do with different incoming water temperatures

The flow follows from the unit power and the rise needed. These figures use 13 kW, about 44,000 BTU per hour, and standard water properties.

IncomingRise to 105 FFlow
Mild, 70 F35 F riseAbout 2.5 gallons per minute.
Cool, 55 F50 F riseAbout 1.8 gallons per minute.
Cold, 45 F60 F riseAbout 1.5 gallons per minute.
Very cold, 40 F65 F riseAbout 1.4 gallons per minute.

Real units vary. Check the manufacturer rating for your model.

Electrical

The electrical side of a 13 kW install

A unit this size usually needs a dedicated 240 volt circuit and a double-pole breaker, with wire sized to match. Many older panels do not have room or capacity, so a panel upgrade or a subpanel may be needed. This work should be done by a licensed electrician or a plumber working with one, and it needs a permit.

We check the panel and the available capacity on the first visit, and we tell you if the electrical work will add significant cost. If it does, a gas unit or a tank may be the better choice.

Fit

Who a 13 kW unit suits and who needs more

Picking the right size matters more than the brand.

A small home or apartment

One bath, short pipe runs and mild incoming water. A single unit can serve a shower plus a sink.

A point-of-use install

A small unit near one fixture, such as a remote bath, can work well.

A family home

Several showers and appliances at once usually need a larger unit or several units, not 13 kW.

Very cold incoming water

Flow drops fast. In winter, a unit that works in summer may deliver only a trickle at full heat.

Gas, tank or a larger electric unit

If the panel cannot support the load, a gas tankless unit, a tank heater or a hybrid heat pump heater may fit better. We explain the trade-offs. See tankless water heater for gas and electric options.

If an existing tank is failing, water heater replacement is the faster path. Either way, a clear price comes before work starts.

Care

Maintenance and expectations

Electric tankless units have no flame, but scale still builds on the elements and heat exchanger. A regular flush and a clean inlet filter keep the unit working. Error codes usually relate to flow, temperature or power.

Do not expect endless hot water at any flow. The unit heats a limited amount of water to a set temperature. Keeping flow at the level the unit supports keeps showers hot.

Questions to answer before you buy a 13 kW unit

Six answers decide whether 13 kW is the right size.

  • How many amps does my panel have free, and is there a space for a double-pole breaker of the size the unit needs?
  • How long is the wire run from the panel to the planned location?
  • What is the coldest incoming water temperature I expect in winter?
  • How many fixtures might run hot water at the same time?
  • Is the unit meant for the whole house or one fixture?
  • Who will pull the permit and schedule the inspection?
Showers

A realistic expectation for showers

A typical low-flow showerhead uses about two gallons per minute. In mild incoming water, a 13 kW unit can supply that and a little more. In cold winter water, the same unit may fall short of a comfortable shower temperature at full flow.

If you buy one anyway, a flow limiter or a low-flow head can keep the water hot. For a whole house, a larger unit or two smaller units is the normal choice. We would rather size the system than sell you a unit that disappoints.

Fernley winters and your 13 kW unit

Fernley is a cold desert, with January lows around 23 degrees on average and colder nights possible. Incoming water in winter can be well below summer temperatures, especially in exposed supply lines, which lowers what a 13 kW unit can deliver.

Permits apply: the Nevada State Contractors Board says new and replacement water heaters need permits and inspections. The City of Fernley Building Department at 775-784-9900 can explain the process, and for electrical changes it can advise on what to expect.

Sources: climate normals for Fernley and Nevada State Contractors Board and City of Fernley Building Department.
