Gas Water Heater Exhaust Pipe Installation for Fernley, NV Homes

The exhaust pipe carries combustion gases outside. It has to be sized, sloped and sealed for the specific heater.

By Fernley 24/7 Plumbing · Published November 16, 2026 · Updated November 16, 2026

A gas water heater makes carbon monoxide and water vapor when it burns fuel. The exhaust pipe, also called a vent or flue, carries those gases out of the house. A bad vent can spill exhaust into the room, which is a serious safety problem. The pipe must match the heater type, rise at the right slope and end where it can discharge safely.

Atmospheric vent heaters use hot-air buoyancy and a metal vent that rises to the roof. Power-vent and direct-vent heaters use a fan and plastic pipe that can run through a wall. Each type has its own rules, and the manufacturer's instructions and local code control.

Types

Common gas water heater vent types

Knowing your type tells you which rules apply.

TypeHow it ventsTypical pipe
Atmospheric (natural draft)Hot gases rise through the vent by buoyancyMetal vent pipe to the roof.
Power ventA fan pushes exhaust outPVC or other approved plastic pipe.
Direct ventSealed combustion, pulls air from outside and vents outConcentric or dual pipe through a wall.
Condensing high efficiencyExhaust is cool and acidicApproved plastic pipe with condensate drain.

Always follow the manufacturer instructions for the model.

What a good vent installation includes

A tidy vent is a safe vent.

  • The right pipe material and size for the heater, from the manufacturer's instructions.
  • A consistent upward slope on natural draft vents, with no sags where condensate can collect.
  • Secure, sealed joints, supported at proper intervals.
  • Clearances from combustibles, and correct termination height or distance from openings.
  • A draft test or a combustion check after startup.
  • Combustion air supply, especially in a small closet or utility room.
  • A carbon monoxide alarm near sleeping areas.

Safety

Warning signs of a venting problem

Venting problems can be silent. These signs are not.

Soot or scorching at the draft hood

Exhaust spilling out instead of rising.

A pilot or burner that goes out often

Poor draft or insufficient air.

Moisture on the vent pipe or nearby walls

Condensation from cool exhaust, or a vent that is too large or leaking.

A carbon monoxide alarm sounding

Leave the house and call emergency services. Do not reenter until it is cleared.

Replacing a heater with a different type

Switching from an atmospheric vent to a power vent or a tankless unit changes the venting. The old metal flue may not work, and the new pipe may need a different route. That can mean wall penetrations, a new roof jack or a condensate line.

See water heater installation for planning, water heater replacement for a swap and tankless water heater if you are changing type.

Professional work

Why venting is not a good DIY project

A vent that looks fine can still spill exhaust. A small error in slope, a joint that is not sealed or a vent that is too long can all cause problems. These faults are not obvious, and the results can be dangerous.

A plumber checks draft with a test after startup, and an inspector confirms that the installation meets code. If you are replacing a heater yourself, the vent is the part to leave to a professional. See water heater installation for how we plan the whole job.

Special cases

Retrofit situations that need extra care

Each of these situations needs a check against the manufacturer instructions.

A heater in a small closet

Combustion air is limited. Louvered doors or ducted air may be needed.

A shared chimney

A heater venting into a chimney with another appliance can have draft problems.

A low-ceiling garage

Clearances to the ceiling and to vehicles must be met.

A cold unheated space

Condensation can form on the vent, and insulation helps.

Testing

How a draft test is done

After startup, a plumber lights the burner and holds a test device or a lit match-free indicator near the draft hood opening. A healthy vent pulls the exhaust up the pipe. If the exhaust spills into the room, the vent or the combustion air needs attention.

The test should be done with doors and windows closed and fans running, since a kitchen exhaust fan or a dryer can pull air the wrong way. If you have such fans, tell the plumber so the test matches real conditions. A passing result in a calm test does not prove the vent is safe on a windy night.

Annual check

Routine checks you can do yourself

Once a year, look at the vent from the heater to where it leaves the house. The joints should be tight, with no gaps or rust streaks. The pipe should not sag. Feel for a draft at the draft hood while the burner runs, with a safe indicator, and stop if you see spillage.

Do not stack items against the vent or the heater. Keep the area clean, and replace carbon monoxide alarm batteries on schedule. Anything unusual is a reason to call, since the cost of a check is small compared with the risk.

Permits and venting in Fernley

New and replacement water heaters need permits and inspections in Nevada per the Nevada State Contractors Board. For a vent change inside Fernley, the Building Department and Code Enforcement can explain what applies at 775-784-9900 on weekdays.

Fernley winters bring January lows near 23 degrees, so a vent in an unheated space may form condensation and frost. Ask the plumber about insulation and routing to reduce that. In a garage, a vent that passes near a vehicle path should be protected.

Sources: Nevada State Contractors Board and City of Fernley Building Department and Code Enforcement.
