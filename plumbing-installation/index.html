Basics

Drain, waste and vent: why layout matters

A fixture is only as good as the drain and vent behind it.

PieceWhat it doesWhat goes wrong
Supply linesBring pressurized water to fixturesLeaks, low pressure, freeze.
Drain linesCarry waste away by gravityWrong slope causes clogs.
Vent linesLet air in so drains flowMissing vents cause gurgling, slow drains.
TrapsHold water that blocks sewer gasDry or siphoned traps let odors in.

A permit and inspection often apply to this work.

Permits and inspections

The City of Fernley Building Department and Code Enforcement handles permits at 775-784-9900 on weekdays, and its permit desk closes at 4 pm. The Online Permit Center is at cityoffernley.org. New water heaters also need permits under state rules, according to the Nevada State Contractors Board.

We explain which parts of your job need a permit and handle the details with you. For supply-line replacements, see repiping.

Local planning

Planning an addition, garage or outbuilding line

New lines to a detached garage or shop need a trench below frost depth, and the right depth depends on local code, so we confirm it with the Building Department rather than guess. Drain lines need slope toward the sewer or septic connection.

For a garage or shop with a floor drain, ask the Building Department what the drain requires before the slab is poured. Talk to us early so utilities, electrical and plumbing are laid out together.

Cost factors

What affects the price of plumbing installation

The price depends on the scope, the distance from existing lines, the type of fixtures, the permit and the amount of wall or floor work.

You get a clear price before work starts. We separate labor, materials and permit so you can see each piece.
