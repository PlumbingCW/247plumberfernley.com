Repiping replaces the water supply pipes in a house or section of it. It is a bigger job than a single repair, so we start with an inspection and explain why it makes sense. If you have an active leak, shut off the main and call the 24/7 line first.

Repiping in Fernley is worth considering when pipes leak repeatedly, water is discolored, pressure has dropped or the pipe material is a known problem. We use modern PEX or copper, plan the routes with you and confirm the price and the permit steps before work starts.

Signs your pipes may need replacing

One leak does not mean repipe. A pattern of failures usually does.

  • Repeated leaks in different spots, especially pinholes in copper or leaks at fittings.
  • Discolored water, such as brown or yellow, when you first open a tap.
  • Low pressure that has built up over time and affects more than one fixture.
  • Pipes of a material with a poor record, such as old galvanized steel or polybutylene.
  • A history of freeze splits or repairs on the same line.
  • Visible corrosion, flaking or green and white crust on fittings.

Process

How a repipe goes

A repipe is organized work, not a surprise.

Inspect and plan

We look at the existing pipe, walls, attic and crawlspace, and plan routes that cause the least damage.

Shut off and rough in

Water is shut off and new lines are run to fixtures. A partial repipe does one zone at a time.

Test and connect

New lines are pressure tested and joined to fixtures and the water heater.

Patch and finish

Access holes are small and located to be patched. We explain what repair is included and what is separate.

Partial repipe or whole house

If the failing section is limited, a partial repipe may be enough. If the pipe material is the problem or leaks keep appearing, replacing everything avoids repeating the visit. We give you both options when it makes sense.

For leak-related questions, see leak detection. If a slab leak is the reason you are calling, slab leak repair covers rerouting.

Local rules

Permits and water quality in Fernley

Model plumbing codes such as the Uniform Plumbing Code and the International Plumbing Code are published by industry bodies, and the code a city enforces is set locally. A repipe typically needs a permit, and the City of Fernley Building Department and Code Enforcement at 775-784-9900 can explain how. Its Online Permit Center is at cityoffernley.org.

If you see discoloration, ask the City of Fernley Utility Billing office at 775-784-9930 about its annual Consumer Confidence Report. It describes water quality at the source. Local pipes can be affected by the water they carry, so we discuss that with you.

Local conditions

Repiping in a cold desert climate

Fernley winters bring January lows near 23 degrees on average. New supply lines in unheated attics, garages and exterior walls need insulation or a different route, because water in them can freeze. We plan routes through conditioned space where possible.

If you are also changing a water heater or adding fixtures, see plumbing installation. A repipe is the right time to add shutoff valves and fix pressure issues.

Cost factors

What affects the price of repiping

The price depends on the number of fixtures, the size and layout of the house, the pipe material, access to walls and the amount of patching.

You get a clear price before work starts. We write down what is included so you can compare it to other bids.
