On-Demand Hot Water Heater Installation in Fernley, NV

An on-demand heater is a tankless heater. The unit is small, but the planning around it is not.

By Fernley 24/7 Plumbing · Published November 16, 2026 · Updated November 16, 2026

An on-demand hot water heater heats water as you use it, instead of storing it in a tank. When you open a tap, flow starts a burner or elements, and hot water follows within moments. It uses less space than a tank and avoids the standby heat loss of a tank that keeps water hot all day.

Installing one is a bigger job than swapping a tank. The unit needs the right gas line or electrical circuit, venting that matches the model and a spot on the wall that meets clearance rules. Planning those items is what keeps the install on budget.

Basics

The three things an on-demand install has to get right

These three items decide whether the unit works as expected.

Fuel supply

A gas unit uses a lot of gas for a short time, so the line size matters. An electric unit draws a heavy load, so the circuit and the panel matter. We check both before ordering.

Venting

Gas units need a vent that matches the model. Some can vent through a wall, others through a roof. A wrong vent causes poor performance and safety issues.

Flow rate and temperature rise

Each unit can heat only so much water by a certain amount. Cold incoming water lowers the flow it can serve, which affects how many fixtures can run at once.

Replacing a tank with an on-demand unit

Switching from a tank to an on-demand unit is the most common reason for a tankless install. The old tank location may not work, because the unit mounts on a wall and needs a vent route. A gas line may need to be larger.

If your tank has failed, see water heater replacement for a faster option. If you are set on tankless, tankless water heater has the details, and water heater installation covers new construction.

Maintenance that keeps an on-demand heater working

A little care goes a long way.

  • Descale or flush the heat exchanger on a schedule based on water quality and the manufacturer's guidance.
  • Clean the inlet filter so flow stays steady.
  • Check the vent and the condensate drain on condensing models.
  • Watch for error codes. They often point to flow, ignition or exhaust problems.
  • Keep the area around the unit clear of storage and debris.
Fuel choice

Gas or electric: how to decide for your home

If your home already has a gas line near the heater, a gas on-demand unit often costs less to run. It also needs a larger line and a vent, which can add install cost. An electric unit has no vent, but it needs a heavy circuit and enough panel capacity.

Ask a plumber to check both. If the panel is full or the gas meter is small, the answer may be a tank, a hybrid heat pump heater or a smaller point-of-use unit near the bathroom. We explain the numbers on site rather than quoting from a phone call.

Surprises

Common surprises during an on-demand install

These are the items that change the price. Knowing them up front helps you budget.

A gas line that is too small

The unit needs more gas at once than a tank does. A larger line may be needed.

No clear vent path

The old flue may not work. A new route through a wall or roof adds cost.

Panel at capacity

An electric unit may need a panel upgrade before it can be installed.

Wall space and clearances

The unit needs space on the wall and clearance around it.

Sizing

Sizing an on-demand heater for your household

Sizing starts with the highest flow you need at once. Count the fixtures that might run together, such as a shower and a dishwasher, and add up their flow rates. Then check the temperature rise needed, which is the target temperature minus the incoming water temperature.

A unit that meets both numbers will keep up in daily use. A unit that meets only one will disappoint. We do this math on site so you do not have to guess from a box label.

Permits and water supply in Fernley

According to the Nevada State Contractors Board, permits and inspections are required for new and replacement water heaters, tankless units included. In Fernley, the Building Department and Code Enforcement can be reached at 775-784-9900, and it also has an Online Permit Center.

Water quality affects tankless service life. The City of Fernley Utility Billing office at 775-784-9930 lists annual Consumer Confidence Reports. Bring the latest to your appointment so we can talk about scale and filters.

Sources: Nevada State Contractors Board and City of Fernley Building Department and Utility Billing.
