Water Heater Dip Tube Replacement in Fernley, NV

A small plastic tube inside the tank sends cold water to the bottom. When it fails, you get lukewarm showers and a heater that seems to run out early.

By Fernley 24/7 Plumbing · Published November 16, 2026 · Updated November 16, 2026

A dip tube is a plastic or metal pipe inside the water heater, attached to the cold water inlet. It carries incoming water to the bottom of the tank so it can be heated. If it cracks or breaks, cold water mixes at the top with hot water, and your hot water runs out sooner or never gets very hot.

Replacing a dip tube is a small repair when the tank is sound. It requires shutting off the water, draining part of the tank, removing the inlet nipple and installing a new tube. If the heater is old or leaking, replacement is a better use of money.

Symptoms

Signs of a failed dip tube

These signs also fit sediment or a failed element, so a plumber confirms before replacing parts.

Lukewarm water from the start

The tank is full of hot water, yet showers never get hot. Cold water is entering at the top.

Hot water runs out quickly

A shorter shower than you used to get can mean the tube is shortened by a break.

White plastic bits in aerators

Small pieces in faucet screens can come from a disintegrating tube.

Pressure and temperature seem fine

The thermostat works and the water is hot at the top, but outlets get mixed water.

How it works

What the dip tube does and why it fails

Hot water rises, so the outlet pipe takes water from the top of the tank. The cold inlet needs to deliver water to the bottom so it can warm up. The dip tube is the pipe that does that job.

Some older plastic tubes break down from heat and water chemistry. Others crack when the heater is moved, flushed or repaired. A tube that is only partly broken still lets cold water leave the inlet too high.

Process

How the replacement is done

It is a short job when the fittings are not corroded.

Shut off and drain

The cold supply is closed, power or gas is turned off and some water is drained from the tank.

Remove the inlet nipple

The nipple on the cold inlet is unscrewed. The dip tube is attached to it or sits inside the tank.

Install the new tube

A new tube of the right length and material is placed, and the nipple is reinstalled with proper sealing.

Refill and test

The tank is filled, air is bled from the lines, power or gas is restored and temperature is checked.

Repair or replace the heater

If the heater is old, rusted at the fittings or has a history of repairs, a new dip tube may be wasted money. Corroded inlet nipples can break during removal and turn a small job into a bigger one.

We tell you what we see and what each path costs before work starts. See water heater repair for the repair route, or water heater replacement if the tank is at the end of its life.

Rule out

Other causes of lukewarm water to rule out

A good diagnosis rules these out first. Replacing a dip tube without testing can leave the real problem in place.

Failed lower element or thermostat

On an electric heater, a dead lower element leaves the top of the tank hot and the bottom cold. Showers run out fast, which looks like a dip tube problem.

Mixing valve or crossover

A leaking mixing valve or a cross-connection in a faucet can feed cold into the hot line. A single faucet that runs lukewarm points there.

Sediment

Layers of scale on the tank floor insulate the water from the burner or element. Flushing helps if the tank is sound.

Thermostat setting

A setting that is too low gives lukewarm water by design. Check it before anything else.

In the meantime

What to do while you wait for a repair

Lukewarm water is a comfort problem, not a safety problem, unless the tank leaks or you smell gas. You can keep using the heater. Run a shower first and short baths later, and avoid running the dishwasher and washer at the same time.

If you see plastic bits in faucet screens, remove and clean the aerators. The pieces can clog small openings. Keep the pieces to show the plumber, since they help confirm the diagnosis and the age of the part.

Permits, code and your water heater in Fernley

According to the Nevada State Contractors Board, new and replacement water heaters require permits and inspections, and contractors who skip them can be disciplined. A dip tube repair on an existing heater is not a replacement, but if a repair turns into a swap, the permit rule applies.

The City of Fernley Building Department and Code Enforcement can explain what applies at 775-784-9900 on weekdays. We tell you before work starts whether a job needs a permit, so there are no surprises.
