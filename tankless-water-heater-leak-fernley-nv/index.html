Tankless Water Heater Leak in Fernley, NV: Causes and Fixes

A leak at a tankless unit is not always the heater. Here is how to tell a loose fitting from a failed part.

By Fernley 24/7 Plumbing · Published November 16, 2026 · Updated November 16, 2026

Plumbing questions?.

Call Now

A tankless water heater leak usually comes from a connection, a condensate drain or an internal part. Shut off the water and power or gas to the unit, dry the area and look for where it drips. A fitting that weeps is a simple repair. A leak from inside the unit often means the heat exchanger or a water-side part has failed.

Because tankless units can be repaired or replaced depending on the cause, a plumber should look before you decide. Do not run the unit with a leak that you cannot find, since water near electrical parts is dangerous.

Urgent situations

First steps when you find water

These steps are safe while you wait for help.

Shut off the cold water

Turn the isolation valve on the cold inlet. Many units have service valves for this.

Turn off power or gas

Switch the breaker off for an electric unit. Close the gas shutoff for a gas unit.

Dry the area and watch

Wipe the unit and watch where drips return. This points to the source.

Check the condensate line

Condensing gas units drain acidic water. A blocked drain can overflow and look like a leak.

Scale

Scale and why it matters

Mineral scale builds on the inside of the heat exchanger and narrows the passages. The unit works harder, overheats and can crack parts over time. Flushing the unit on a schedule removes scale, and a softener or filter can slow buildup.

If you do not know your water quality, test your tap and ask your water utility for its latest report. We do not guess at hardness. For more on how we service these units, see tankless water heater.

Repair or replace a leaking tankless heater

A leak at a fitting or a valve is a simple repair, and the unit can last many more years. A leak from the heat exchanger or a corroded cabinet is usually a replacement, because the part is costly and the unit has already shown wear.

We tell you what we see and what each path costs before work starts. For repair work, see water heater repair.

Prevention

Preventive care that avoids leaks

Most tankless leaks start small. A weeping fitting, a loose condensate line or a slightly scaled heat exchanger shows up first as dampness or an error code. Catching it early turns a replacement into a repair.

Once a year, look at the unit with a flashlight. Check the isolation valves, the inlet filter and the vent joints. Wipe the unit dry, wait a day and look again. A flush with a descaling solution on the schedule the maker recommends reduces scale. These small steps cost little and extend the life of the unit.

The visit

What a plumber brings to a tankless leak call

We carry isolation valve kits, fittings, gaskets, condensate parts and a descaling pump for the common models. For a leak at a fitting or valve, the repair is often done in one visit. For an internal leak, we confirm the diagnosis and explain the replacement options.

We also check the inlet pressure, the gas supply or the electrical load, and the vent. A leak can have a cause upstream, such as high water pressure, and a new part will fail if the cause stays. You get a clear price before work starts.

Water quality and permits in Fernley

The City of Fernley Utility Billing office at 775-784-9930 lists annual Consumer Confidence Reports, covering fiscal years 2020 through 2024 on its site. Reviewing the latest report helps you understand what flows through your tankless unit.

New and replacement water heaters, tankless units included, need permits and inspections in Nevada according to the Nevada State Contractors Board. The City of Fernley Building Department can explain the process at 775-784-9900.
