Plumbing Rough-In for a Slab in Fernley, NV

Pipes under a slab get one chance to be right. This guide explains what the rough-in includes and why timing matters.

By Fernley 24/7 Plumbing · Published November 16, 2026 · Updated November 16, 2026

Plumbing questions?.

Call Now

A plumbing rough-in for a slab sets the drain lines, stub-ups and sleeves before concrete is poured. After the pour, changing them means cutting concrete, which is costly. The rough-in has to match the plans, the fixture locations and local code, and it usually needs an inspection before the slab is covered.

If you are building a new home, an addition or a garage with a bathroom, bring the plumber into the conversation before the forms are set. A short planning meeting can prevent a costly change after the pour.

Process

The order of work for a slab rough-in

The sequence matters more than any single step.

Plan fixture locations

Toilets, tubs, showers and floor drains are marked. Positions of walls and cabinets are confirmed so drains land where they need to.

Dig and lay drain lines

Trenches are cut at the right slope. Pipe is laid, joined and supported, and cleanouts are placed where they can be reached later.

Set stub-ups and sleeves

Pipes that will rise through the slab are capped and labeled. Sleeves protect lines that cross the concrete.

Test and inspect

The drain lines are tested, usually with water or air, and an inspection is arranged when the jurisdiction requires it.

Pour the slab

Only after the work is approved should the concrete go in.

Questions to settle before the pour

These questions are cheaper to answer now than after the pour.

  • Where do the toilets, tubs, showers and floor drains go, and are the wall locations final?
  • Where will the water heater sit, and does it need a drain or a gas line to be stubbed?
  • Where will cleanouts be installed so the line can be cleared without breaking concrete?
  • Will the house need space for future fixtures, such as a basement bath or an outdoor kitchen?
  • Which inspections are required before the slab is poured, and who schedules them?
  • Do the plans show the slope and size of drains, and do they match the fixtures?

Materials

Choosing materials and thinking about the long term

Drain lines in a slab are usually plastic pipe, and supply lines may be routed through walls or attics instead of under the slab. Supply lines under slabs have a track record of issues in some homes, so many builders now avoid putting them there.

If you are planning a water heater, think about where the drain and relief valve discharge will go. See plumbing installation for the work that follows the rough-in, and water heater installation if the heater is part of the plan.

Coordination

Working with your builder or contractor

If a general contractor is in charge, ask them how plumbing is scheduled relative to site work and forms. Plumbers need access before concrete trucks arrive, and a delay in one trade can push the others. A short call between the plumber, the builder and the inspector avoids surprises.

Share the final fixture plan and any changes right away. A moved toilet, a larger tub or a new floor drain is easier to handle at the rough-in stage than after the pour. If you are the owner-builder, ask the Building Department what documents you need to bring to inspection.

Final check

What the final walkthrough should confirm

A short checklist at the end of rough-in is worth the time.

Stub-up heights and positions

Pipes should line up with the wall and fixture plans, within tolerance.

Caps and protection

Open pipes should be capped to keep concrete and debris out.

Cleanout locations

Marked, reachable and not buried in landscaping later.

Test results

Drain lines hold water or air pressure as the inspector requires.

Future changes

A note on future flexibility

A slab freezes your plumbing layout. If there is any chance you will add a bathroom, a laundry or a garage sink, install extra capped stub-outs or a larger drain now. It costs little at rough-in and saves a lot later.

Ask the plumber about a second cleanout and about vent routing that leaves room for changes. A few extra fittings at this stage are cheap insurance. See plumbing installation for how we handle later additions.

Permits and inspections in Fernley

The City of Fernley Building Department and Code Enforcement handles permits and inspections. It is open on weekdays and can be reached at 775-784-9900. The permit desk closes at 4 pm. An Online Permit Center is available at cityoffernley.org.

Ask the department which inspections are required before the slab is poured, and book them early so concrete is not delayed. A plumber familiar with the process can help you line up the schedule.
