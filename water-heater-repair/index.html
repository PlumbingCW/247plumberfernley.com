Water heater repair in Fernley covers failed heating elements, thermostats, gas valves, pilot assemblies, relief valves and leaking fittings. When the tank itself has failed, we tell you straight away that replacement is the answer and explain the options.

Urgent situations

What to do before the plumber arrives

Some water heater problems are safety issues. These steps keep people safe until help arrives.

Leak at the tank

Close the cold supply valve on top of the heater. Switch the breaker off for an electric unit, or turn the gas control to pilot or off for a gas unit.

Smell of gas

Leave the house and call the gas utility from outside. Do not light anything or flip switches. Then call us once it is safe.

Hot water that is scalding

A stuck thermostat can overheat the tank. Turn the heater off and keep the relief valve clear. Do not plug the relief valve.

Rust-colored or smelly water

Cloudy or rusty hot water can point to a corroding tank or anode. Note it for the plumber.

Decision

Repair or replace: how that decision gets made

A heater with a failed thermostat, element or pilot assembly is usually worth repairing if the tank is sound. A leak from the tank body is the end of the line, since the steel liner has corroded through. An older unit with sediment, rust and multiple repairs in a row is also a candidate for replacement.

We tell you what we see and what each path costs before work starts. If the tank has failed, see water heater replacement for what that involves. If you are leaning toward a different type of unit, tankless water heater explains that option.

Types

Gas, electric and tankless repairs

We repair the common types of water heater in homes and small businesses.

Gas storage tank

Pilot and thermocouple faults, gas control valves, flue draft problems and burner cleaning.

Electric storage tank

Heating elements, thermostats, high-limit switches and wiring faults.

Tankless units

Error codes, scale buildup and flow sensor faults. Fernley residents can ask us about flushing a tankless unit.

Relief and expansion devices

The temperature and pressure relief valve and the expansion tank are safety parts. We test them on each visit.

Rules

Permits, code and who can do the work

New and replacement water heaters need a permit and an inspection in Nevada, according to the Nevada State Contractors Board, and a contractor who skips them can be disciplined. A repair that does not replace the unit usually does not need a permit, but swapping the tank does.

The City of Fernley Building Department and Code Enforcement can be reached at 775-784-9900 on weekdays. It also runs an online permit center. We explain which parts of a job need a permit before we start, so nothing is a surprise.

Safety

Gas safety and flue issues

A gas water heater needs fresh air and a clear flue to burn properly. A blocked or backdrafting flue can push exhaust into the house, which is why we check draft when we service a gas unit.

Keep storage and cleaning products away from the heater. If you ever smell gas or see soot around the draft hood, leave the area and call for help. The U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission advises placing carbon monoxide alarms outside each sleeping area in homes with fuel-burning appliances.

Cost factors

What affects the price of water heater repair

The price depends on the part, the type of unit, how easy it is to reach and the hour of the call. A thermostat swap is a different job from a gas control valve replacement.

You get a clear price before work starts. If the repair would cost a large share of a new unit, we say so and let you decide.
