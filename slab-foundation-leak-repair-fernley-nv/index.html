Slab Foundation Leak Repair for Fernley, NV Homes

A leak under the slab calls for a calm, step-by-step response: stop the water, find the leak, then choose the repair.

By Fernley 24/7 Plumbing · Published November 16, 2026 · Updated November 16, 2026

Plumbing questions?.
(855) 575-2890
Call Now

If you think you have a slab leak, shut off the main water valve first. That stops the damage while you plan. Then call a plumber to locate the leak, because repair choices depend on where it is and why it happened. Do not start cutting concrete on a guess.

Slab leak repair usually follows one of three paths. A spot repair opens the slab over the leak. A reroute abandons the leaking line and runs new pipe through walls or the attic. A repipe replaces failing lines more broadly. The right path depends on the pipe, the number of leaks and the house.

Process

A sensible order of action

Each step avoids a common mistake.

Shut off the main

Turn the valve fully closed. Open a faucet to relieve pressure.

Document the signs

Photograph the meter, wet spots and warm floors. Note when you first noticed each.

Locate the leak

A plumber uses listening equipment, thermal imaging and pressure tests to narrow down the spot.

Choose the repair

Review spot repair, reroute and repipe options with the price of each.

Dry and restore

After repair, dry the area and check for mold risk before closing walls or floors.

Risks

What a slab leak can damage

Damage grows quietly under concrete.

Flooring

Tile can crack and lift. Wood buckles. Carpet and pad hold moisture.

Soil under the slab

Leaking water can wash fines out and leave voids.

Walls and baseboards

Moisture wicks up and leaves stains, swelling and mold.

Your water bill

A steady leak can run for weeks. See leak detection if you suspect one.

Insurance and documentation

Coverage for slab leaks varies by policy. Many policies cover resulting damage and the cost of access, but not the pipe itself. Call your insurer before repair work and ask what they require.

We provide a written description of the leak and the repair for your records. For related repair details, see slab leak repair and repiping.

Expert help

Why a leak under a slab is not a DIY job

Concrete is hard to open and expensive to repair. A guess at the leak spot can lead to a hole in the wrong place and a bigger bill. Detection tools find the spot within a few inches, which keeps the opening small.

Pressure testing separates a supply leak from a drain leak, and thermal imaging tells hot lines from cold. A plumber who owns the tools and reads them often can save you from a repair at the wrong spot. If you suspect a leak, shut off the main and make the call.

Damage

Signs the leak has already done damage

If you see these signs, mention them when you call so the plumber looks for them.

New cracks in walls

Soil movement under the slab can shift framing and drywall.

Doors that stick

A sagging slab edge changes door alignment.

Musty smell

Moisture trapped in the subfloor or walls feeds mold.

Sunken or damp soil near the foundation

Leaking water can wash soil away or keep it saturated.

Deciding

Choosing between repair paths: a short guide

If one leak shows up in a pipe that is otherwise sound, a spot repair is usually the lowest cost. If the line is old and has leaked before, a reroute or a repipe removes the risk of another leak under concrete.

Think about how long you plan to stay. A repipe is a sound investment for a long-term home. For a rental you will sell soon, a spot repair may be enough. We explain the pros and cons without pressure and give a clear price before work starts.

Before you approve

Questions to ask a plumber before approving a slab repair

Ask how the leak was located and whether you can see the evidence. Ask what the repair will cost under each option and what is included. Ask what happens to the flooring and who restores it. Ask how long the work will take and when water will be on again.

Ask about the warranty and what it covers. A plumber who explains each of these without pressure is a good sign. If the answer to any of them is vague, ask again, and get a second opinion if you are not comfortable.

Fernley ground water and slab leaks

The January 5, 2008 Truckee Canal breach flooded about 580 homes on Fernley's east side and led to a $18.1 million settlement from the Truckee-Carson Irrigation District. New homes there were built above street level. Ground water here is not a theoretical issue.

If a slab edge or crawl area stays damp, ask for a leak check before assuming it is ground water. For permit questions on slab repairs, the City of Fernley Building Department can be reached at 775-784-9900.
